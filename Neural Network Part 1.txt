 Neural network

A neural network consists of neurons, just like our brain cells. 
It consists of input layers, hidden layers, and output layers. 
The connections between each neuron have weights and bias. 
The weights are transferred from one neuron to the other neurons. 
The bias is designed to adjust the value to get a desired output based on the data. 

Gradient descent

Gradient descent is an optimization algorithm which is used to find the minimum of a cost function. 
It goes by calculating the current gradient, which is a slope at its current position, 
 and moves towards the opposite side to find the local minima. 
Depending on the current parameter and learning rate, 
 the algorithm may settle into different local minima positions. 
Ultimately, this algorithm will find a model parameter which produces the lowest loss possible. 

Backpropagation

Backpropagation takes place in an artificial neural network. 
Backpropagation is a process of adjusting the weights and bias whenever we get bad performance in prediction or an incorrect prediction, 
because we only have influence over the bias and weight.

Backpropagation works on the concept of the chain rule, which has an influence over previous neurons' weights and biases, 
and then ends up using a technique called stochastic gradient descent to find the model parameters which produce the lowest loss.

Stochastic gradient descent

Stochastic gradient descent is an optimization algorithm, just like gradient descent, 
but it works concisely and produces the shortest route to reach its local minima. 

This overall process makes the model adjust and get better results and predictions with minimal loss. 

There is a saying in neurons, which is popular amongst developers, 
which goes as "neurons that fire together, wire together." 
This is also known as Hebb's law. This was quoted by neuroscientist Dr. Carla Shetz.

Word embeddings

Word embeddings are dense numerical vectors that represent words in a continuous vector space to give a semantic and syntactic meaning.

Vector space

Similar words are arranged together in a multidimensional vector space because they share similar meanings.

ML inputs

Words are converted into tokens because the computers can't generally understand words. It only understands numerical vectors, 
so words are converted into vectors for better understanding by computers.

Semantic math

Vectors allow operations such as finding similar concepts of words using semantic math. 
For example, king denotes as male. To find a similar word, such as queen, the vectors need to find a distance between word vectors, 
such as man and woman. After they find that distance between those vectors, the other vectors, such as queen, 
will be found in the same area as queen. That is called semantic math. 

Cosine similarity

To find a semantic relationship between words, we need cosine similarity because it shows the distance in numerical form. 
When the cosine similarity number is positive, the words are closer. When it is zero, the words are perpendicular. 
When it is negative, the words are located further than the current word.

Embedding matrix

An embedding matrix consists of loaded vocabularies as vectors in numerical form 

context size

Context size is the information stored, and it processes them within a range. 
For example, a context size of 2048 means only 2048 tokens are processed in a single sequence.
